JournalGuard AI: testing 100% of journal entries¶

AI & Finance · Project 2 · Team 9 (ESSEC). Report generated from the pipeline's outputs.

Question. Among all journal entries of a ledger, which ones are at risk of fraud or material error? We score every entry instead of a sample, and write an audit memo for each alert. A score prioritises audit work; it is not a probability of fraud, and the auditor decides.

1. Data¶

DIB General Ledger Data (Gronewald, Stephan, Rombach, Fettke; Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22014653, CC BY 4.0): a synthetic German company (SKR03 chart); the original file has 850 labelled anomalies in 8 scenarios (the datacard below shows the file used for this run). The generator's helper columns (weekend, nwh, promptly, top_n, high_cash, label, marking) are dropped on load: they would let a model cheat. Enrichment: public holidays (Nager.Date API, holidays library as fallback), the ledger's own working day, user hour profiles, account age, rare account combinations, materiality from revenue.

3. Method¶

  1. Rules (points): P1 timing (weekend, holiday, year-end, revenue at year end, late entry, outside working hours), P2 amount (round, just below the approval threshold, split, above performance materiality, high cash), context (rare account pair, new account, unusual hour for the user, duplicates). Timing counts fully only for manual entries.
  2. Unsupervised: Isolation Forest, LOF and an autoencoder (MLP). LOF or the autoencoder replaces Isolation Forest only with a clear gain (+0.02 PR-AUC); otherwise we keep the simpler model.
  3. Supervised: XGBoost on the features and who entered the entry, decision cut tuned on validation rows, explained per entry with SHAP. Person 2's HistGradientBoosting model is a further layer.
  4. Composite risk score 0-100 (noisy-OR of the layers); alert at 50.

Evaluation: temporal split (the last 25% of the period is never trained on) and a circularity test: two scenarios are never shown to XGBoost.

6. Generative AI¶

A local LLM (Ollama, so client data never leaves the laptop) is optional. Without it, every memo is built from Person 3's recommendation library, grounded only in computed facts. Uses: cleaning and categorising entry descriptions (nature), plain-language explanation of each alert, grouping of alerts by theme, audit memo per alert, engagement summary, natural-language questions about the alerts.

7. Limitations¶

  • The ledger and its labels are synthetic; real fraud can look different and real ledgers are confidential.
  • Labels are a sample of entries matching each pattern, so precision has a ceiling; rules alone are weak here.
  • Circularity: the supervised model only finds what it was taught (see the held-out scenarios).
  • Thresholds and materiality are our assumptions (pipeline/config.py).
  • Gen AI can produce plausible but wrong explanations: memos use only computed facts, SHAP shows the real drivers, and every memo must be reviewed. Human judgment remains mandatory.
